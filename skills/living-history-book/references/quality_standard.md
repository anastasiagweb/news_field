# Quality Standard — Five Tests Every Story Must Pass

Read before framework design, before drafting, before every audit, and whenever a story starts to feel flat. Each Living History story must pass five independent tests. A story is not "good overall"; it passes each test on its own, and strength in one never compensates for weakness in another.

| Test | Question | Primary auditor roles |
|---|---|---|
| **Gripping** | Will a native adult reader want to finish it — and then read the next one? | Story Engine & Plausibility; Native Reader |
| **Alive** | Is it real literature — specific, felt, shaped, restrained? | Prose Stylist; Dialogue; Native Reader |
| **True** | Is everything in it possible and accurate for its world, including the minds? | Fact-Checker; Mentalité; Onomastics |
| **Clear** | Can a B1–B2 learner read it with pleasure? | CEFR Register |
| **Standalone** | Does it work alone, in any order, with no other story read? | Standalone & Orientation |

---

## 1. Gripping

The native reader has no obligation to finish. The story must earn every page.

**The engine.** Every story has a question the reader wants answered, posed within the first 200 words and kept alive until near the end: Will she get the jar to the shrine before the storm? Who took the silver? Will he tell his father? What is in the sealed room? Will the stranger be allowed to stay? A story that is only a sequence of tasks, however sensory, has no engine.

**Stakes that matter to someone.** Not world history — a person's loss or gain: a debt, a marriage, a child's health, a reputation, a place in the house, a favour with a god. Small stakes, felt strongly, beat big stakes stated.

**Turns.** At least two moments where the situation changes direction. A story that moves in a straight line from problem to solution is flat.

**Withholding.** Not everything at once. The reader reaches forward.

**Failure signals:** the protagonist meets no real resistance; the problem is solved by someone else or by luck; the outcome is obvious from the opening; the middle is a catalogue of sensory description; the reader could stop at the midpoint without missing anything.

**Test:** stop reading at the midpoint. Do you want to know what happens? Write the question you want answered. If you cannot, the story has no engine.

## 2. Alive

The line's stories are literature that happens to be readable at B1–B2, not graded readers dressed in history.

**Concrete observation.** The world lives in the specific thing noticed: not "a busy market" but "a woman shouting the price of figs over a basket of eels". Not "a beautiful jar" but "a jar with a crack in the glaze that she turned to the wall". Every scene should contain at least one detail that could belong only to this story.

**Shown feeling.** Emotion through body, action, silence, and object — not labels. "She folded the cloak and put it away without looking at it" carries more than "She was sad." A labelled emotion now and then is fine; labelling as a habit is a Major finding.

**Subtext.** Moments where the reader feels more than the sentence says. Two lines placed side by side; a question not answered; a gift refused.

**Rhythm.** Sentence lengths vary; paragraphs vary; scenes vary. Read aloud in the mind.

**Character.** Each named character has at least one specific physical detail, one speech habit or verbal tic, and one want. Three details make a person; none make a type.

**Restraint.** The story knows when to stop explaining. It trusts the reader. It ends on an image, an act, or a line — not a summary.

**Failure signals:** generic adjectives (*beautiful, ancient, mysterious, precious, sacred* as decoration); dead metaphors; emotions named; scenes that could be in any story; endings that explain; the formula phrases in `anti_formula.md`.

**Test:** choose any paragraph at random. Is there one thing in it you have never read before? If not, it is generic.

## 3. True

Three layers, all mandatory:

- **Facts.** Every object, practice, place, name, and event fits the time and place, per `fact_check_doctrine.md`.
- **Minds.** Every thought and value fits the time and place, per `mentalite_doctrine.md`.
- **Names.** Every name belongs, per `onomastics.md`.

A story can be gripping and alive and still fail here — and then it fails. A true story that is dull also fails. Both at once is the job.

**Test:** imagine the best specialist on this period reading the story. Where would they wince? Fix that place.

## 4. Clear

The learner at the declared band reads with pleasure: sentences inside the architecture of `cefr_register_<LANG>.md`, no more than the allowed number of period terms, every term clear from context, no abstract vocabulary spikes, no fake-archaic or modern-colloquial English.

Clarity is not simplicity of meaning. The native reader should never notice the band. If a passage feels thin, the fix is never to reach up a band — it is to look harder at the world and write a sharper concrete sentence inside the band.

**Test:** read one page as a B1–B2 learner would. Is there any sentence you would have to read twice for its grammar (not for its meaning)? Fix it.

## 5. Standalone

The story works for a reader who opens the book at that page.

- Place, time, and world are clear within 150–200 words, through concrete detail.
- No reliance on another story's events, characters, or explanations.
- No spoilers of another story.
- Any link to another story is a bonus, invisible to the single-story reader.
- The ending resolves the story's own question (or leaves it open by design, not by dependence).

**Test:** read the story as if it were the only one in the book. Is anything confusing, missing, or pointing elsewhere?

---

## How the tests interact

- When **True** and **Gripping** collide (the exciting plot is impossible), True wins and the plot is rebuilt. There is always a true plot that grips; the past was full of trouble.
- When **Clear** and **Alive** collide (the vivid sentence is above the band), find a sentence inside the band that is just as vivid. Concrete nouns are allowed; complex syntax is not.
- When **Standalone** and a planned **link** collide, drop the link.
