# Anti-Formula — Banned Moves, Banned Phrases, Quotas

Read before framework design, before drafting each story, before every audit, and before the publish-ready edit. This is the line's defence against the one-template book.

Formula is the silent killer of a collection. One weak sentence is a blemish; ten stories with the same arc, the same coda, and the same phrases are a dead book. The lists below are enforced mechanically — scan for them — and judged by ear. They are bans, not models: never imitate anything listed here.

---

## Part 1 — Banned structural moves

Each may appear **at most the number of times shown per book** (0 = never).

| # | Move | Max per book |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Opening at waking or daybreak | 1 |
| S2 | An authority demands an impossible task by a near deadline | 1 |
| S3 | Crisis solved by a sudden improvisation that turns out better than the plan | 1 |
| S4 | An authority figure watches and praises the protagonist | 1 |
| S5 | Payment multiplied, official recognition, or a seal of approval as reward | 0 |
| S6 | Knowledge passed to the next generation as the closing beat | 1 (only if it is the story's actual subject) |
| S7 | Coda about cycles, seasons, generations, continuity | 0 |
| S8 | Closing that looks ahead to tomorrow's work and settles into tonight's rest | 0 |
| S9 | Narrator foresight of later history, or of modern discovery of the past | 0 |
| S10 | Sneering official or scribe as antagonist | 1 per series |
| S11 | An elder's aphorism that states the theme | 0 as ending; 1 per book in the body |
| S12 | A character explains the story's moral or the meaning of their work in a speech | 0 |
| S13 | Mentor shows apprentice a secret, then apprentice succeeds | 2 (as central relationship) |
| S14 | The protagonist alone at night reflecting on the day | 1 |
| S15 | Rival turned friend through shared craft at the end | 1 |
| S16 | A foreigner or expert admits the local product is better | 0 |
| S17 | Ending on an epiphany of understanding, maturity, or deep satisfaction | 0 |
| S18 | Closing list of everything the story showed | 0 |

## Part 2 — Banned phrases and phrase families

Never use, in narration or dialogue. Variants and paraphrases count. The families below are stated in English; each language reference (`per-language/<lang>.md`) lists the same families as they appear in its language, and they are banned in the same way. Each occurrence is a finding (Minor; Major if two or more in one story; Critical if the book shows a pattern).

**Generations and continuity**
- generation after generation; passed down through generations; from hand to hand; father's father; back beyond counting; a link in a chain; the chain must not break; as it always had, as it always would; the ancient rhythm; the cycle continued

**Eternity and grandeur**
- eternal (as decoration); timeless; immortal (as praise); for centuries to come; older than any palace; outlasting kings

**Value clichés**
- worth more than its weight in gold or silver; worth more than most families saw in a year or a lifetime; worth a fortune; priceless

**Work and time clichés**
- the work of seven days in three; the palace wanted double; the impossible order; there was no time; every moment counted

**Machine-prose tics**
- a testament to; a tapestry of; a symphony of; a dance of / between; the weight of [abstract noun]; something shifted; for the first time in his/her life (as a beat); a breath she didn't know she was holding; couldn't help but; little did they know; in that moment; the air was thick with; the scent of X mixed with Y; a mixture of X and Y (for emotions); eyes widened; heart pounded or raced; a smile tugged at; silence stretched; time seemed to slow; every fibre of; palpable; intricate; delicate balance; deep satisfaction; a sense of [noun]; she realised that (as an epiphany device); it was more than X, it was Y; not just X but Y (as a reflex); whispered secrets; sacred trust; ancient compact

**Aphorism patterns**
- "X is the mother of Y"; "the difference is what you do with it"; "every X teaches you something"; "X is just Y's way of Z"; any sentence built to be quoted as a lesson

**Modern idiom** (also a mentalité issue; see `mentalite_doctrine.md`)
- no pressure; deal with it; focus; stress; process; team; manage; handle; issue; efficient; efficiency; proposal; projections; percent; deadline; schedule; upgrade; feedback

## Part 3 — Repetition limits across a book

- No distinctive image or simile may appear in two stories.
- Watched words — secret, impossible, sacred, ancient, precious, mysterious — at most three times each per book; any other distinctive word at most three times unless it is a plain everyday word or a necessary period term.
- No two stories may share a closing-paragraph structure.
- No two stories may share a first-sentence structure.
- Invocations of gods are period vocabulary, not repetition — but no more than one story per book ends on a prayer.

## Part 4 — How to scan

The drafter scans each story after writing it; the auditors scan the whole book; the publish-ready edit scans again.

1. Search the text for every phrase in Part 2 and obvious variants.
2. Count each structural move in Part 1 across the book, story by story, in a table.
3. List the first sentence and last paragraph of every story side by side; compare structures.
4. Count watched words across the book.
5. Read every ending aloud: does it end on an image, an act, or a line — or on a summary, a reflection, or a moral?

## Part 5 — What to do instead

- Instead of a coda: end on a concrete object, gesture, or line that holds the story's feeling.
- Instead of praise from authority: let the outcome be ambiguous, costly, private, or noticed only by someone who matters to the protagonist.
- Instead of an impossible order: let the trouble come from inside the protagonist's life.
- Instead of an elder's aphorism: let the elder act.
- Instead of a value cliché: state what the thing is worth in that world's own terms, from the dossier.
- Instead of decorative grandeur: cut it.
