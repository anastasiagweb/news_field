# Claim Taxonomy — What to Extract and Check

Twenty-four categories of claim for premodern and ancient settings. Every research and fact-check phase uses this list: the dossier builder to know what to research, the claim extractor to know what to pull from a text, the fact-checker to choose a verification strategy, and the NOT-YET builder to walk every category for traps.

Every claim gets one primary category. Extract generously: an unextracted claim is an unchecked claim.

For each category: what it covers, how it typically fails, and where to verify.

---

## A. The material world

### 1. Technology and technique
Tools, machines, processes, crafts, construction and lifting methods, metallurgy, firing, pressing, milling, weaving, dyeing, writing media, locks and fastenings.
*Typical failures:* a later technique placed earlier; the logic of a later material applied to an earlier one; a modern craft custom projected back.
*Verify in:* history-of-technology scholarship, excavation reports, peer-reviewed experimental archaeology, museum records of actual tools.

### 2. Materials and their origins
Metals, stones, pigments, woods, fibres, dyes, resins, vitreous materials, ivory, amber, precious stones — and where they came from in this period.
*Typical failures:* a material not used in this place and period; a wrong source region; a luxury material in a humble household.

### 3. Food and drink
Crops, animals eaten, dishes, cooking methods, meal times, drinks, sweeteners, spices, preservation.
*Typical failures:* foods from another continent or a later period; domestic animals before their arrival; meal patterns from another culture.

### 4. Flora, fauna, landscape, climate
Plants, animals wild and domestic, their presence in this region at this time, seasons, weather, agricultural calendar, coastlines and rivers as they then were, forests.
*Typical failures:* animals that did not live there; domestic animals before domestication or introduction; the modern landscape projected back.

### 5. Clothing, adornment, hair, body
Garments, fabrics, colours, footwear, jewellery, hairstyles, cosmetics, body modification, nudity norms.
*Typical failures:* garments of another period or culture; colours from dyes not yet available; dress of the wrong social level.

### 6. Housing and architecture
Building materials, house plans, roofing, floors, windows, doors, lighting, heating, water supply, drainage, public and sacred buildings, fortifications.
*Typical failures:* building features before their attested date; sacred buildings where only open-air cult places existed.

### 7. Transport, travel, navigation
Ships, sails, oars, carts, chariots, pack and riding animals, roads, bridges, travel times, sailing seasons, navigation methods, harbour structures.
*Typical failures:* navigation aids and harbour structures from later periods; riding where only driving existed; impossible travel times.

## B. Measures and money

### 8. Money, exchange, prices
Coinage or its absence, weighed metal, barter, rations, credit, debts, interest, prices, wages.
*Typical failures:* coins before coinage; modern financial reasoning; prices with no period basis.

### 9. Units and measures
Length, weight, volume, area, distance; their names in this culture or neutral words of the book's language.
*Typical failures:* modern or foreign units in narration; a later unit projected back where uncertain.

### 10. Time and calendar
Divisions of the day, weeks, months, years, festivals, seasons, eras; how age was counted.
*Typical failures:* clock time where there were no clocks; weeks where none existed; invented festivals presented as attested; our-era dates in dialogue.

## C. Society and institutions

### 11. Social order and status
Ranks, classes, free and unfree, foreigners, dependants, kinship, household structure, inheritance, women's legal and economic position, children's roles.
*Typical failures:* modern equality; status categories from another period; modern life-stage categories.

### 12. Government, offices, titles
Rulers, councils, assemblies, officials, scribes, priestly offices; their titles in the period and the correct rendering in the book's language.
*Typical failures:* titles from later periods; offices invented as fact; modern bureaucratic roles in period dress.

### 13. Law, justice, punishment
Disputes, courts, judges, oaths, ordeals, witnesses, contracts, seals, penalties, vengeance and settlement, debt bondage.
*Typical failures:* punishments the period did not use; policing institutions; written law before writing or before its attested date.

### 14. Economy and work
Agriculture, herding, crafts, trade, markets, palace or temple economies, labour obligations, slavery, wage labour, workshops, apprenticeship.
*Typical failures:* market economies projected onto redistributive systems; modern labour relations; invented regular institutions presented as fact.

### 15. Warfare and weapons
Weapons, armour, tactics, ranks, fortifications, siege methods, military service, raiding, piracy.
*Typical failures:* equipment and tactics from later periods; cavalry or gear before their attested use.

## D. Mind and culture

### 16. Religion and ritual
Gods attested for this period and place, cults, festivals, priesthoods, sacrifice, divination, purification, magic, funerary practice, afterlife beliefs.
*Typical failures:* a god or cult not yet present here; later mythology projected back; invented festivals presented as attested; titles used as names.

### 17. Writing, literacy, record-keeping
Scripts, who could write, media, what was written, archives, letters.
*Typical failures:* writing in a period or place without writing; wrong writing media; widespread literacy; invented writing practices presented as common.

### 18. Language and naming
Languages spoken, terms for things, personal names (see `onomastics.md`), place names in period form, names of peoples.
*Typical failures:* later or foreign names for peoples and places; modern political units; names from myth or celebrity.

### 19. Knowledge, medicine, science
Medical theories and treatments, herbal knowledge, astronomy, mathematics, geography, beliefs about the body and nature.
*Typical failures:* modern medical or scientific logic; navigation or measurement methods from later periods; knowledge of later discoveries.

### 20. Art, music, literature, performance
Styles with their dates, instruments, songs and epics and when they took the form we know, theatre, games, sports.
*Typical failures:* mixing styles of different centuries; quoting texts before their composition; later instruments.

### 21. Mentalité claims
Attitudes, values, emotions as framed by characters or narrator. Checked against `mentalite_doctrine.md` and, where possible, against sources on the period's values.
*Typical failures:* any of the modern-leak families.

## E. Events, places, people

### 22. Places and geography
Settlements existing at this date, sites, regions, distances, directions, what can be seen from where, sea routes.
*Typical failures:* settlements before their foundation; wrong orientation of landmarks; coastline changes ignored.

### 23. Events and chronology
Wars, destructions, foundations, migrations, natural disasters, reigns and their dates, with chronology debates noted.
*Typical failures:* events out of order; contested dates presented as certain; a famous event pulled into a story's window.

### 24. Real persons
Any real, named historical person (cameo only — see `line_doctrine.md`): dates, offices, whereabouts, what they actually did.
*Typical failures:* a real person in the wrong place or time; invented speech or deeds.

## Extraction checklist (for every paragraph and every Note)

- Every **object** in a hand or in a room → 1, 2, 5, 6.
- Every **food, animal, plant, weather note** → 3, 4.
- Every **number, distance, duration, price, time of day** → 8, 9, 10.
- Every **title, office, rank, institution** → 11, 12, 13, 14, 15.
- Every **god, rite, omen, prayer, burial** → 16.
- Every **act of writing or reading** → 17.
- Every **name, place name, people name** → 18, 22, 24.
- Every **explanation of how something works** → 1, 19.
- Every **style, song, game** → 20.
- Every **thought about society, justice, gods, change, the future** → 21.
- Every **"why" about real events** → 23.
- **Every sentence of every Historical Note, the introduction, the timeline, and the afterword** → extracted as individual claims.
