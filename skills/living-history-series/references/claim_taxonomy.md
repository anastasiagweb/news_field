# Claim Taxonomy — What to Extract and Check

Twenty-four categories of claim for premodern and ancient settings. Every research and fact-check phase uses this list: the dossier builder to know what to research, the claim extractor to know what to pull from a text, the fact-checker to choose a verification strategy.

Every claim gets one primary category. Extract generously: an unextracted claim is an unchecked claim.

---

## A. The material world

### 1. Technology and technique
Tools, machines, processes: potter's wheels (hand-turned vs kick wheel), kilns and firing methods, metallurgy (smelting, alloying, casting, forging, quenching, hardening), presses, mills and querns, looms, dyeing, glass, writing media, locks and keys, lifting devices, surveying.
*Failure modes:* later technique placed early (rotary olive mill in the Bronze Age; enfleurage or distillation of perfume in antiquity); steel logic applied to bronze; a modern craft myth (gold-filled cracks; "dew hardens the edge").
*Sources:* history-of-technology works, excavation reports, experimental archaeology in peer-reviewed venues, museum records of actual tools.

### 2. Materials and their origins
Metals, stones, pigments, woods, fibres, dyes, resins, glass, faience, ivory, amber, lapis lazuli, obsidian, purple; and where they came from in this period.
*Failure modes:* wrong pigment (lapis for Minoan blue instead of Egyptian blue or glaucophane); wrong source region; luxury material in a humble household.

### 3. Food and drink
Crops, animals eaten, dishes, cooking methods, meal times, drinks (wine, beer, mixed drinks), sweeteners, spices, preservation.
*Failure modes:* New World foods in the Old World before 1492 and vice versa; sugar, citrus, tea, coffee, rice where not yet present; chickens before their arrival; meal patterns from another culture.

### 4. Flora, fauna, landscape, climate
Plants, animals (wild and domestic), their presence in this region at this time, seasons, weather, agricultural calendar, coastlines (which may have moved), rivers, forests.
*Failure modes:* animals that did not live there (bears on Crete); domestic animals before domestication or introduction (riding horses, camels, cats, chickens); the modern landscape projected back.

### 5. Clothing, adornment, hair, body
Garments, fabrics, colours, footwear, jewellery, hairstyles, cosmetics, tattooing, body modification, nudity norms.
*Failure modes:* garments of another period or culture; colours from dyes not yet available; trousers or boots where not worn.

### 6. Housing and architecture
Building materials, house plans, roofing (tiles, thatch, flat roofs), floors, windows, doors, lighting, heating, water supply, drainage, public buildings, temples, fortifications.
*Failure modes:* roof tiles before roof tiles; glass windows; chimneys; temples where only open-air altars existed.

### 7. Transport, travel, navigation
Ships (types, sails, oars), carts, chariots, pack animals, roads, bridges, travel times, sailing seasons, navigation methods, ports and harbour structures.
*Failure modes:* lighthouses, sea charts, compasses, latitude by instrument; riding where only driving existed; impossible travel times.

## B. Measures and money

### 8. Money, exchange, prices
Coinage (or its absence), weighed metal, barter, rations, credit, debts, interest, prices, wages.
*Failure modes:* coins before coinage; "a copper" as a tip; percentages; prices with no period basis.

### 9. Units and measures
Length, weight, volume, area, distance; their names in this culture or neutral English.
*Failure modes:* miles, pounds, feet, acres in narration of non-English cultures; a later unit (talent, drachma) projected back where uncertain.

### 10. Time and calendar
Hours, watches, days, weeks, months, years, festivals, seasons, eras; how age was counted.
*Failure modes:* minutes and clock hours; seven-day weeks; "solstice games" invented as fact; our-era dates in dialogue.

## C. Society and institutions

### 11. Social order and status
Ranks, classes, free and unfree, foreigners, dependants, kinship, household structure, inheritance, women's legal and economic position, children's roles.
*Failure modes:* modern equality; status categories from another period (hoplites in the Bronze Age; guilds in antiquity); "teenagers".

### 12. Government, offices, titles
Kings, chiefs, councils, assemblies, officials, scribes, priests' offices; their titles in the period and the correct English rendering.
*Failure modes:* titles from later periods; offices invented as fact; modern bureaucracy ("inspector", "supervisor", "licence") in modern dress.

### 13. Law, justice, punishment
Disputes, courts, judges, oaths, ordeals, witnesses, contracts, seals, penalties, vengeance and settlement, debt bondage.
*Failure modes:* prisons as punishment where not used; police; written law before writing or before its attested date; "trial by jury" anachronisms.

### 14. Economy and work
Agriculture, herding, crafts, trade, markets, palace or temple economies, labour obligations, slavery, wage labour, workshops, apprenticeships.
*Failure modes:* market economies projected onto redistributive systems; "licensed traders"; monthly markets invented as fact; modern labour relations.

### 15. Warfare and weapons
Weapons, armour, tactics, ranks, fortifications, siege methods, military service, raiding, piracy.
*Failure modes:* hoplite gear and phalanx in the Bronze Age; stirrups and cavalry where not used; later shield or helmet types.

## D. Mind and culture

### 16. Religion and ritual
Gods (names attested for this period and place), cults, festivals, priesthoods, sacrifice, divination, purification, magic, funerary practice, afterlife beliefs.
*Failure modes:* a god or cult not yet present (Pan in Thessaly c. 1000 BCE; Demeter where unattested); invented festivals presented as attested; later mythology projected back; titles used as names (Pythia).

### 17. Writing, literacy, record-keeping
Scripts, who could write, media (clay, papyrus, wax, wood, stone, bark), what was written, archives, letters.
*Failure modes:* writing in a period without writing (Greece c. 1100–800 BCE); Phoenicians writing on clay tablets; widespread literacy; "personal notes" invented as common practice.

### 18. Language and naming
Languages spoken, terms for things, personal names (see `onomastics.md`), place names in period form, ethnic names.
*Failure modes:* later or foreign names for peoples and places ("Phoenicians" for the 15th century BCE; "Afghanistan"; "Greece" as a political unit); mythic names on ordinary people.

### 19. Knowledge, medicine, science
Medical theories and treatments, herbal knowledge, astronomy, mathematics, geography, beliefs about the body and nature.
*Failure modes:* germ theory; antivenom logic; navigation by measured latitude; awareness of later scientific facts.

### 20. Art, music, literature, performance
Styles (with dates), instruments, songs and epics (and when they took the form we know), theatre, games, sports.
*Failure modes:* style mixing across centuries (Kamares ware with Marine Style octopuses); quoting a fixed epic text before it was composed; later instruments.

### 21. Mentalité claims
Attitudes, values, emotions as framed by characters or narrator. Checked against `mentalite_doctrine.md` and, where possible, against sources on the period's values.
*Failure modes:* any of the modern-leak families.

## E. Events, places, people

### 22. Places and geography
Settlements (existing at this date?), sites, regions, distances, directions, views (what can be seen from where), sea routes.
*Failure modes:* a city that did not yet exist (Alexandria in the Bronze Age; Metapontion before its founding); sunrise over a mountain that lies to the west; coastline changes ignored.

### 23. Events and chronology
Wars, destructions, foundations, migrations, natural disasters, reigns; and their dates (with chronology debates noted).
*Failure modes:* events out of order; contested dates presented as certain; a famous event pulled into a story's window.

### 24. Real persons
Any real, named historical person (cameo only — see `line_doctrine.md`): dates, offices, whereabouts, what they actually did.
*Failure modes:* a real person in the wrong place or time; invented speech or deeds; famous names in the wrong century.

## Extraction checklist (for every paragraph and every Note)

- Every **object** in a hand or in a room → 1, 2, 5, 6.
- Every **food, animal, plant, weather note** → 3, 4.
- Every **number, distance, duration, price, time of day** → 8, 9, 10.
- Every **title, office, rank, institution** → 11, 12, 13, 14, 15.
- Every **god, rite, omen, prayer, burial** → 16.
- Every **act of writing or reading** → 17.
- Every **name, place name, people name** → 18, 22, 24.
- Every **explanation of how something works** (craft, body, sea, sky) → 1, 19.
- Every **style, song, game** → 20.
- Every **thought about society, justice, gods, change, the future** → 21.
- Every **"why" about real events** → 23.
- **Every sentence of every Historical Note, the introduction, the timeline, and the afterword** → extracted as individual claims.
