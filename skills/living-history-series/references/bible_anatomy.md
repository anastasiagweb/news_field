# Series Bible Anatomy

Read before drafting (P3), auditing (P4), and revising (P5) the series bible. The bible is the single source of truth for a series: every book pitch and every book run reads it. It must be complete enough that a book run never guesses about the civilisation's basics, and honest enough that its own facts survive a fact-check.

Target length: **9,000–16,000 words**, in the series' language, as adult working prose and tables. Every factual statement carries an evidence status (`fact_check_doctrine.md`) and a source key.

---

## §1 Series identity
Series title; one-paragraph promise; civilisation and span; language; CEFR band; number of books; what the series deliberately does not do.

## §2 Line rules as applied here
Pointer to the shared doctrine; series-specific adjustments, each justified (protagonist quotas the evidence cannot support, darkness specifics for practices attested in this civilisation, the cameo list).

## §3 Chronology and periodisation
Master chronology table (period names, dates with competing chronologies, defining changes, main sites); the series' dating convention for front matter; the book windows from the locked concept, each with dates, place, social world, evidence density.

## §4 The world — civilisation reference
One subsection per domain, written for writers, with evidence status on every non-trivial claim and with explicit notes on **how each domain changes across the book windows** (tables by window preferred):

1. Geography and landscape.
2. Peoples, languages, scripts, literacy.
3. Political order.
4. Social order.
5. Women, men, children, old age.
6. Economy and work.
7. Material life.
8. Technology.
9. Religion and ritual.
10. Law, justice, conflict.
11. Knowledge, medicine, time-keeping, measurement.
12. Arts, music, games, storytelling.
13. Contact with other peoples.

## §5 Mentalité guide
Built on `mentalite_doctrine.md` and made specific to this civilisation: what people took for granted; how feelings were framed; what was funny; how belief and practice changed across windows; and a series-specific list of at least fifteen things a person of this world would never think or say.

## §6 Onomastics
Sources of names by window with keys; transliteration convention; strategy for unknown names if needed; name patterns by sex and status; forbidden-name list; an initial reserve pool of at least forty attested names per book window, split by sex and status.

## §7 Rendering policy
How titles, offices, gods, places, units, money, time, distances, and peoples are rendered in English; when a period term is used; a starter rendering table of at least forty rows.

## §8 Sensory and material palette
Per window: characteristic sounds, smells, colours, textures, foods, materials, light and darkness — every item checked.

## §9 Story-world envelope
Which forms of `story_forms.md` this civilisation's evidence supports especially well and which it cannot support (with reasons); realism anchors for plotted forms by window, sourced; darkness specifics and respect notes, including living communities and restricted knowledge; real-figure cameo policy and permitted cameo figures by window.

## §10 Myths and misconceptions to avoid
Popular errors about this civilisation, each with the evidence-based correction and source keys; at least ten for a widely known civilisation.

## §11 Apparatus conventions
Dating format; how Notes refer to evidence; how the introduction handles invented names; afterword conventions; spelling and typography choices beyond the register pack.

## §12 Series-level NOT-YET backbone
Anachronism traps for the whole series by window, with earliest attestation per region; at least sixty entries for a long series.

## §13 Internal glossary
Terms writers will need, with rendering and one-line meaning. Not printed in books.

## §14 Sources
Bibliography by tier with keys, URLs, access dates. Every key used in the bible appears here.

---

## Quality bar

- **Complete:** every section present; nothing left open at PASS.
- **Sourced:** load-bearing claims on two independent Tier A–C sources; uncontroversial ones on one Tier A/B source.
- **Honest:** evidence status visible; debates recorded.
- **Usable:** a book run finds what it needs in minutes; tables wherever they serve.
- **Even to the end:** the last sections are as complete as the first.
