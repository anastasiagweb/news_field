# Series Bible Anatomy

Read before drafting (P3), revising (P5), and auditing (P4) the series bible. The bible is the single source of truth for a series: every book pitch and every book run reads it. It must be complete enough that a book run never has to guess about the civilisation's basics, and honest enough that its own facts survive a fact-check.

Target length: **9,000–16,000 words**, in the series' language. The bible is a working document for writers and auditors, not for readers; it does not need to be in the CEFR band. Every factual statement carries an evidence status (`fact_check_doctrine.md`) and a source key.

---

## §1 Series identity
- Series title; one-paragraph promise (no superlatives).
- Civilisation and overall span covered; language; CEFR band; number of books.
- What the reader gets across the series; what the series deliberately does not do.

## §2 Line rules as applied here
- Pointer to the shared doctrine (`line_doctrine.md` and companions).
- Series-specific adjustments, each justified: e.g., protagonist quotas the evidence cannot support (no named female scribes known), darkness specifics (attested human sacrifice: how it is shown), real-figure cameo list.

## §3 Chronology and periodisation
- Master chronology table: period names (scholarly), dates (with competing chronologies where relevant), key changes, main sites.
- The series' chosen dating convention for front matter (e.g., "around 1450 BCE"; which chronology).
- The book windows (from the locked concept), each with date window, place, social world, evidence density.

## §4 The world — civilisation reference
One subsection per domain, written as a reference for writers, with evidence status on every non-trivial claim. For each domain, note **how it changes across the book windows**.

1. Geography and landscape (regions, routes, climate, seasons, agricultural calendar, coastline changes).
2. Peoples, languages, scripts, literacy (who could read and write what, when).
3. Political order (rulers, titles, councils, administration) per window.
4. Social order (ranks, free/unfree, foreigners, kin, household, inheritance).
5. Women, men, children, old age — roles, rights, rites, by window and class.
6. Economy and work (agriculture, herding, crafts, trade, palace/temple economies, labour obligations, markets or their absence, money or its absence).
7. Material life (houses, light, water, food and drink, clothing, furniture, tools).
8. Technology (by window — what exists, what does not yet).
9. Religion and ritual (gods attested per window, cult places, festivals, priesthoods, divination, purity, death and afterlife).
10. Law, justice, conflict (disputes, oaths, ordeals, penalties, feud, war, raiding, piracy).
11. Knowledge, medicine, time-keeping, measurement.
12. Arts, music, games, performance, storytelling (with dates of forms and styles).
13. Contact with other peoples (who they met, traded with, feared; what they called them).

## §5 Mentalité guide for this civilisation
Built on `mentalite_doctrine.md`, made specific:
- What people took for granted (gods, fate, honour, kin, the dead, the body, time, outsiders).
- Emotional vocabulary and how feelings were framed (body, gods, honour/shame).
- Humour: what was funny.
- Ten to twenty things a person of this world would **never** think or say (series-specific leak list).
- How belief and practice changed across the windows.

## §6 Onomastics
- Sources of names by window (`onomastics.md`), with keys.
- Transliteration convention.
- Strategy for unknown names (if needed), with the rule.
- Name patterns by sex and status.
- Forbidden-name list for this civilisation.
- Initial reserve pool: at least 40 names per book window (attested), split by sex and status.

## §7 Rendering policy — how we name things in English
- Titles and offices (e.g., "the king" vs a period term; when the term is used).
- Gods' names (spelling; when epithets are used).
- Places (period names vs modern names; modern names only in Notes and front matter).
- Units, money, time, distances (period terms or neutral English; never miles, pounds, minutes in stories).
- Peoples and foreigners (period names or neutral descriptions; never later ethnonyms).
- A starter rendering table (thing → rendering → evidence/status), extended by each book's dossier.

## §8 Sensory and material palette
Per window: characteristic sounds, smells, colours, textures, foods, materials, light and darkness — all period-true, all checked. This feeds concrete observation; it is not a list of clichés.

## §9 Story-world envelope
- Which forms of `story_forms.md` this civilisation's evidence supports especially well (e.g., legal disputes for Classical Athens; letters for Old Babylonian merchants; curse tablets for Roman provinces), and any it cannot support (with reason).
- Realism anchors for plotted forms: the kinds of trouble attested in this civilisation (with sources).
- Darkness specifics and respect notes (`respect_and_darkness.md`), including living communities and restricted knowledge.
- Real-figure cameo policy and the list of permissible cameo figures per window.

## §10 Myths and misconceptions to avoid
Popular errors about this civilisation, each with the evidence-based correction and source key (e.g., for Greece: marble statues were white; all Greeks were democrats; Spartans threw weak babies off a cliff as routine — and what the evidence actually supports).

## §11 Apparatus conventions
- Dating format; how Notes refer to evidence; how the introduction handles invented names; afterword style.
- Spelling conventions (from the register pack) and any series-specific choices.

## §12 Series-level NOT-YET backbone
A master list of anachronism traps for the whole series, by window, with earliest attestation per region (each book's dossier will extend its own list). At least 60 entries for a long series.

## §13 Internal glossary
Terms writers will need, with rendering and one-line meaning (not printed in books).

## §14 Sources
Bibliography by tier with keys, URLs, access dates. Every source key used in the bible appears here.

---

## Bible quality bar

- **Complete:** every section present; nothing left as "TBD" at PASS.
- **Sourced:** load-bearing claims with ≥2 independent Tier A–C sources; uncontroversial ones with ≥1 Tier A/B.
- **Honest:** evidence status visible; debates recorded, not hidden.
- **Usable:** a book run can find what it needs in minutes; tables preferred over essays where possible.
- **Consistent to the end:** the last sections are as complete as the first (the earlier generation's plan collapsed into fragments in later volumes — this bible must not).
