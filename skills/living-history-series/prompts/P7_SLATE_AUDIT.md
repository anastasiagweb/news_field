# P7 — SLATE AUDIT (panel + fact-check)

You are the **Slate Audit Panel** — nine independent roles — and, separately, the **Fact-Checker** for the slate's anchors and premises. You decide whether these story concepts are worth building books on. You do not revise; P8 revises.

Read first, fresh: `references/audit_taxonomy.md`, `references/story_forms.md`, `references/story_card_craft.md`, `references/anti_formula.md`, `references/quality_standard.md`, `references/mentalite_doctrine.md`, `references/onomastics.md`, `references/fact_check_doctrine.md`, `references/claim_taxonomy.md`, `references/respect_and_darkness.md`, `references/failure_gallery.md`, `templates/audit_report_schema.md`, `templates/fact_check_report_schema.md`. Read `slate.vN.md`, `name_registry.md`, `bible.md`, `01_survey.md`, the line registry (if any), and the previous slate audit (if any).

---

## Part A — Fact-check (`slate_factcheck.vN.md`)

For every story concept, extract and check:
- the anchor(s): does the evidence exist, in this window and place, and does it show what the slate says?
- the premise's factual preconditions: the occupation existed; the setting existed at that date; the trouble is of an attested kind; the season fits the activity (harvests, sailing seasons, festivals);
- the protagonist's status/occupation/sex combination is plausible for the window;
- any real person or event named.

Bible-verified facts may be confirmed by reference to the bible's keys; new facts need sources per `fact_check_doctrine.md`. Verdict: PASS iff zero Critical and zero Major.

## Part B — Panel (`slate_audit.vN.md`)

| # | Role | Code | Looks for | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Variety & Coverage | VAR | Every book's variety contract (recount it yourself — do not trust the slate's table); series coverage of people | 0 C, 0 M |
| 2 | Series Uniqueness | UNQ | Duplicate concepts, anchor reuse, form distribution across books, line-registry repetitions | 0 C, 0 M |
| 3 | Story Engine | ENG | Is each engine a real question? Would a native reader want the answer? Are there turns implied? Flag theme-not-story and task-list concepts | 0 C, 0 M |
| 4 | Plausibility | PLA | Plotted concepts against the plausibility contract (scale, motive, means, coincidence); elite-level intrigue ≤1 per book | 0 C, 0 M |
| 5 | Mentalité | MEN | Premises that need modern minds, modern institutions, or modern values; protagonist wants framed in modern terms | 0 C, 0 M |
| 6 | Onomastics | ONO | Every name against `onomastics.md`, the bible's forbidden list, the registry; confusable pairs in planned casts | 0 C, 0 M |
| 7 | Standalone | STA | Concepts that depend on other stories; links that would confuse a single-story reader | 0 C, 0 M |
| 8 | Native Reader | NAT | Read each book's ten loglines as a browsing reader: would you buy this book? Which three concepts are weakest? (Weak concepts in a book = Major) | 0 C, 0 M |
| 9 | Respect & Darkness | RES | Concepts that would require gore, sexual content, exoticism, stereotype, restricted knowledge | 0 C |

**Fact-check (Part A):** 0 C, 0 M.

## Procedure
1. Part A first.
2. Recount every book's variety table yourself.
3. Read the slate three times: as a browsing native reader (book by book), with notes per role, and for targeted checks (quotas, uniqueness, names).
4. Write the report per the schema, with per-book quota tables and series tables.
5. Stuck-loop check; gate verdict.

Loop cap: 4. Update `00_state.md` (`next_phase: P8` or checkpoint at PASS).

## Checkpoint at PASS
Message the user (their language): the slate is ready; three to five highlights; path to `slate.md`; ask them to choose the pitch mode (all books now / a batch / just-in-time) and whether they want to swap any concept before pitches.
