# Respect and Darkness — How Hard, How Honest, How Respectful

Read before series design, story cards, frameworks, and every respect audit. The line writes about hundreds of civilisations, many of them with living descendants and living religions. It writes about slavery, war, sacrifice, hunger, and death because they were there. This document sets the ceiling for darkness and the floor for respect.

---

## Audience

Adult readers — native speakers and adult learners. Books should also be safe to give to a thoughtful older teenager. That sets the ceiling.

## The darkness ceiling

**Allowed and expected, shown honestly:**
- Slavery and unfreedom as daily reality: work, sale, punishment, separation of families, escape, manumission.
- War and raiding: fear, flight, siege, loss, captivity.
- Death in every form: illness, childbirth, accident, violence, sacrifice where attested, execution.
- Hunger, disease, disaster, poverty, debt.
- Harsh law and harsh customs of the period.

**How it is shown:**
- Violence happens **mostly off the page**: we see before and after, sounds, consequences, faces. A blow may land on the page; wounds are described briefly and without lingering.
- **No gore**, no torture shown on the page, no lingering on suffering for effect.
- **Human sacrifice** (where attested: e.g., some Mesoamerican, Shang, and other contexts): shown from inside the culture's meaning, with restraint, never as spectacle or horror-show; never invented where not attested.
- **Sexual content:** none beyond the implied. Marriage, desire, love, adultery, prostitution, and sexual exploitation of the unfree may exist in the story world; they are treated with discretion, never described, never eroticised. No sexual content involving minors in any form, including implication.
- **Children** may suffer the realities of their world (work, illness, loss, slavery), never shown as victims of violence on the page.
- **Animals:** sacrifice, hunting, and slaughter were everyday; show them plainly and briefly.

**Not allowed:** cruelty as entertainment; shock endings for their own sake; a story whose main pleasure is suffering.

## The respect floor

1. **From the inside.** Every culture is written from the inside, by the standards of its own people's dignity. No "primitive", "savage", "barbaric", "exotic", "mysterious East", "strange customs" framing — in narration or in Notes.
2. **No noble savage either.** Idealising a culture is also a failure: no peaceful utopias where evidence shows conflict; no matriarchal paradises where evidence is ambiguous.
3. **Beliefs are not jokes.** Characters may joke about their gods as their culture did; the text never mocks the beliefs. Rituals are not "superstition" in the narrator's voice.
4. **Living religions and living descendants.** Where a culture's religion or traditions are still practised (e.g., Hindu, Shinto, Jewish, Indigenous traditions of the Americas, Australia, the Pacific, Africa), the series bible must:
   - identify the living communities;
   - avoid portraying sacred figures in ways those communities would find disrespectful;
   - **never reveal restricted or secret-sacred knowledge** (e.g., Aboriginal Australian restricted ceremonies), even if some of it has been published;
   - prefer sources that include the community's own scholarship and perspectives.
5. **No stereotypes.** Check every foreigner, minority, and ethnic group in a story for stereotype: the greedy trader, the cruel barbarian, the sly foreigner. Foreigners are individuals.
6. **Enslaved people are people.** Never furniture, never only victims: they have names (where the period gave them names), wants, skills, humour, and choices — inside their constraints.
7. **Modern politics stays out.** Do not map present-day nations, conflicts, or identity politics onto the past. Use the period's names for peoples and places in stories.

## Real people

Real historical persons appear only as background or brief cameo (see `line_doctrine.md`). They are never shown committing invented crimes, never given invented sexual lives, and never ridiculed.

## Severity guidance

- **Critical:** gore or torture on the page; sexual content beyond implication; any sexualisation of minors; contempt for a culture or religion; restricted sacred knowledge revealed; a stereotype that defines a group.
- **Major:** violence lingered on; an exoticising narrator; an enslaved character used only as a prop; a minor stereotype; an idealised utopia contrary to evidence.
- **Minor:** a single word with exoticising flavour ("strange", "exotic") in narration.

The respect gate in every audit is **zero Critical** (Majors require an accept-as-is rationale or fix).
