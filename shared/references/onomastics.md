# Onomastics — Names That Belong

Read before allocating any name (series bible, slate, book pitch, framework, draft) and before every onomastics audit. Names are the first thing a reader meets and the cheapest place to lose their trust. A name from the wrong century, the wrong culture, or a famous myth tells the native reader that nobody checked.

---

## The five tests every name must pass

1. **Period and place.** The name was used (ideally attested) in this civilisation, in this window, in this region, in this language. A name common in one period of a civilisation is not automatically right for another period or region of it.
2. **Sex and status.** The name fits the person's sex and social position. Many cultures marked slaves, foreigners, priests, or elites with distinctive name types.
3. **No celebrity shadow.** The name does not carry a famous person, god, hero, or myth into the reader's mind — unless the name was demonstrably common in that world *and* the bible allows it.
4. **Not a god, title, or place.** No god's name as a personal name unless the culture did that; names built from a god's name are fine where attested. No titles used as names. No place names as personal names unless attested as such.
5. **Unique in the series.** No name is used twice in the same series, for any character, in any book, unless the bible declares a deliberate exception flagged in the registry. The **name registry** enforces this.

## Readability rules for learners and natives

- **Name budget.** At most five named characters per story (protagonist included). Others are referred to by role.
- **Distinct shapes.** No two named characters in one story share an initial letter or a similar sound shape. Different lengths help.
- **Pronounceable.** Prefer names of two or three syllables for protagonists. Longer names are allowed when they are the period norm; then use an attested short form if one exists, or the full name consistently.
- **One spelling.** The series bible fixes one transliteration convention (letters, endings, diacritics). Never mix within a series.
- **No diacritic overload.** Use the simplest standard transliteration the bible allows; reserve diacritics for the Historical Note if needed.

## Where names come from

Each series bible must name its onomastic sources, found and verified during the survey: name lexica and prosopographies for the civilisation; corpora of inscriptions, tablets, papyri, charters, and administrative records; and, with caution about their date of writing, literary sources. Always check the date of the source against the story's window: a source written down centuries later is evidence for naming in its own time first.

## When names are unknown

For civilisations whose ordinary names are lost, the bible must choose and declare one of these strategies, in this order of preference:

1. **Names recorded by neighbours** who wrote about this people, used as a small, honest pool.
2. **Bynames and descriptive names** rendered in plain words of the book's language, used sparingly where the culture plausibly used such names; better for minor characters than for protagonists.
3. **Rule-based invented names** that follow the sound patterns of attested words or neighbouring languages, declared in the bible with the rule, and acknowledged once per book in the introduction.

Never present a reconstructed or invented name as attested in a Historical Note. Never borrow the names of a later culture that conquered or replaced this one without saying so — and prefer not to.

## Name registry

Every series keeps a registry (`name_registry.md`, schema in templates). Each entry records name, sex, book and story, role, status, attestation (source and date range, or the invention rule), and meaning if known and useful.

The slate allocates all protagonist and main secondary names; book pitches and frameworks add minor names from the reserve pool; drafts may not introduce a new name without adding it to the registry and passing the onomastics check.

## Forbidden-name list

Each series bible maintains a forbidden list for its civilisation: gods, heroes, mythical figures, famous historical persons, famous artists and writers, and titles. Exception only if attested as a common name in the window **and** approved in the bible.

## Severity guidance

- **Critical:** a famous person's or god's name given to an ordinary protagonist; a name from the wrong civilisation or a much later period; a name reused for a different character within the same series without declared exception.
- **Major:** an unattested name presented as attested in a Note; a name of the wrong sex or status type; a mythic-hero name on a secondary character; two characters in one story with confusable names; inconsistent spelling convention.
- **Minor:** a plausible but unverified name not yet in the registry; a name that is hard to pronounce where an easier attested alternative exists.
- **Nit:** a single spelling slip.

The onomastics gate in every audit is **zero Critical, zero Major**.
