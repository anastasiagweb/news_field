# Quality Standard — Five Tests Every Story Must Pass

Read before framework design, before drafting, before every audit, and whenever a story starts to feel flat. Each Living History story must pass five independent tests. A story is not "good overall"; it passes each test on its own, and strength in one never compensates for weakness in another.

| Test | Question | Primary auditor roles |
|---|---|---|
| **Gripping** | Will a native adult reader want to finish it — and then read the next one? | Story Engine & Plausibility; Native Reader |
| **Alive** | Is it real literature — specific, felt, shaped, restrained? | Prose; Dialogue; Native Reader |
| **True** | Is everything in it possible and accurate for its world, including the minds? | Fact-Checker; Mentalité; Onomastics |
| **Clear** | Can a learner at the declared band read it with pleasure? | CEFR Register |
| **Standalone** | Does it work alone, in any order, with no other story read? | Standalone & Orientation |

---

## 1. Gripping

The native reader has no obligation to finish. The story must earn every page.

- **The engine.** Every story has a question the reader wants answered, posed within the first 200 words and kept alive until near the end. A sequence of tasks, however sensory, is not an engine.
- **Stakes that matter to someone.** A person's loss or gain, felt strongly. Small stakes felt strongly beat large stakes stated.
- **Turns.** At least two moments where the situation changes direction.
- **Agency.** The protagonist makes at least one decisive choice; the resolution comes from the protagonist's action, knowledge, or choice — not from luck, and not from an authority's decree.
- **Withholding.** Not everything at once; the reader reaches forward.

**Failure signals:** no real resistance; the problem solved by someone else or by luck; an outcome obvious from the opening; a middle made of description; a midpoint where a reader could stop without missing anything.

**Test:** stop at the midpoint and write the question you want answered. If you cannot, the story has no engine.

## 2. Alive

The line's stories are literature that happens to be readable at the declared band, never graded readers in period dress.

- **Concrete observation.** The world lives in the specific thing noticed. Every scene contains at least one detail that could belong only to this story.
- **Shown feeling.** Emotion through body, action, silence, and object. A labelled emotion now and then is acceptable; labelling as a habit is a Major finding.
- **Subtext.** Moments where the reader feels more than the sentence says.
- **Rhythm.** Sentence lengths, paragraph lengths, and scene modes vary.
- **Character.** Each named character has at least one specific physical detail, one habit of speech or gesture, and one want.
- **Restraint.** The story trusts the reader and ends on an image, an act, or a line — never on a summary.

**Failure signals:** generic adjectives used as decoration; dead metaphors; named emotions; scenes that could belong to any story; endings that explain; anything listed in `anti_formula.md`.

**Test:** choose a paragraph at random. Is there one thing in it you have never read before? If not, it is generic.

## 3. True

Three layers, all mandatory:

- **Facts** fit the time and place (`fact_check_doctrine.md`).
- **Minds** fit the time and place (`mentalite_doctrine.md`).
- **Names** belong (`onomastics.md`).

A gripping, alive story that fails here fails. A true story that is dull also fails. Both at once is the job.

**Test:** imagine the leading specialist on this period reading the story. Where would they wince? Fix that place.

## 4. Clear

The learner reads with pleasure: sentences inside the architecture of `per-language/<lang>.md`, period terms within budget and clear from context, no abstract vocabulary spikes, no fake-archaic or modern-colloquial language.

Clarity is not simplicity of meaning. The native reader should never notice the band. If a passage feels thin, the fix is never to reach up a band — it is to look harder at the world and write a sharper concrete sentence inside the band.

**Test:** read one page as a learner at the band would. Is there any sentence you would have to read twice for its grammar? Fix it.

## 5. Standalone

- Place, time, and world are clear within 150–200 words, through concrete detail.
- No reliance on another story's events, characters, or explanations; no spoilers of another story.
- Any link to another story is a bonus invisible to the single-story reader.
- The ending resolves the story's own question, or leaves it open by design, never by dependence.

**Test:** read the story as if it were the only one in the book. Is anything confusing, missing, or pointing elsewhere?

---

## How the tests interact

- When **True** and **Gripping** collide, True wins and the plot is rebuilt. There is always a true plot that grips; the past was full of trouble.
- When **Clear** and **Alive** collide, find a sentence inside the band that is just as vivid. Concrete nouns are allowed; complex syntax is not.
- When **Standalone** and a planned **link** collide, drop the link.
